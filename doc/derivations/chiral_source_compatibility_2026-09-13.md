### Internal chirality and interpretation

Assume the supplied self-adjoint odd Dirac operator D, Γ†=Γ, Γ²=I, {Γ,D}=0, the entire isolated kernel P of positive chirality, a bounded smooth real scalar h commuting with Γ, and the reduced inverse D_Q^−1 on the complement. U commutes with Γ, so {Γ,UDU†}=0, with conjugate domain U Dom(D), unchanged spectrum/index and transported kernel UPU†. Smooth multiplication preserves the usual sphere Dirac domain. Leakage relative to the old P is not chirality loss. Common phase multiplication of both bra and ket cancels in Gram/scalar overlaps, but does not compute the one-sided compression S.

In contrast {Γ,D+εM_h}=2εΓM_h and the first-order zero-cluster shifts are the eigenvalues of εB. The constant source shifts all modes equally, not splitting their degeneracy; the quadratic source preserves twofold degeneracy. Vanishing first-order shift is not protection to all orders. The formal second-order contribution is −ε²P M_h Q D_Q^−1 Q M_h P=0: all factors except the inverse preserve chirality, the inverse reverses it, and the final positive-kernel projection annihilates it. Removing only part of the kernel, using positive-energy-only denominators or replacing the inverse by |D_Q|^−1 destroys the argument. G is a denominator-free leakage Gram, not an energy correction. No nonzero-mode matrices or full Dirac solver are constructed, and no absence of higher-order shifts is claimed.
