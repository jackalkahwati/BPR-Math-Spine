## Reviewed condensate expectation and variational equation

The complete ket has coefficients psi_n=sqrt(N!/product_x n_x!) product_x u_x^n_x in lexicographic fixed-number occupations. Empty powers are1. The multinomial theorem normalizes it; a_x|u;N>=sqrt(N)u_x|u;N-1> gives <a_x†a_y>=N conjugate(u_x)u_y and <n_x(n_x-1)>=N(N-1)|u_x|^4. The Berry term is iN<u,u_dot>. Variation with normalized u gives i u_dot=Ku+g(N-1)|u|²u+lambda(t)u, where real lambda fixes phase. ## Reviewed phase convention

The bridge derivation identifies a necessary distinction before numerical evaluation. Uniform Hartree u(t)=exp(-i mu t)u0 has mu=-2C+g(N-1)/L. Its N-particle ket carries exp(-iN mu t), whereas the mean-energy trial evolution carries exp(-iEt) with E=-2CN+gN(N-1)/(2L). Multiplication by exp(+i gN(N-1)t/(2L)) converts the former to the latter. The proposed Hilbert-norm bound min(2,|t|sigma) compares exact evolution to the mean-energy phase, not the uncorrected Hartree ket. Independent adversarial derivation confirms both phase and variance. In first-quantized uniform positions, D counts equal-site unordered particle pairs. Each indicator has variance(L-1)/L²; disjoint pairs are independent and overlapping pairs have zero covariance since a triple equality has probability1/L². Hence Var D=binom(N,2)(L-1)/L². Uniform hopping acts as scalar -2CN on this ket, so (H-E)|psi>=g(D-<D>)|psi> without assuming hopping commutes with D. Duhamel integration gives the corrected bound. The uncorrected Hartree ket instead has residual sqrt(sigma²+U²), U=gN(N-1)/(2L), and cannot use sigma alone for this Hilbert-norm comparison. No numerical result is inferred from the reviewed identity.
